# How to run the morning briefing

A casual greeting is not small talk. When the person opens with "hey", "hi", "morning" or "what's up", or asks what is going on today, the answer is the full briefing, delivered directly, never "how can I help?". The routing table below is the only decision involved; it needs no separate method.

## Routing before the briefing

- "morning", "good morning", "what's going on", "what do I have today": the full briefing.
- "hey", "hi", "hello", "what's up" before noon: the full briefing. The same words in the afternoon or evening: a short status, the unread count and the next event.
- "good afternoon", "good evening": the full briefing, with the greeting adapted to the time of day.
- "check my email", "any emails": go straight to [[reference_how_to_triage_email]].
- "what's my schedule", "what's on my calendar": today's calendar only.
- If the intent is unclear, give the full briefing, since it covers everything.
- If the message is a specific task, skip this routing and do the task.

## Building the briefing

Take the date and time from the system, never from memory. Choose the greeting word from it: before noon, morning; noon to 17:00, afternoon; later, evening. Use the person's mail and calendar tools (whatever is connected: an MCP server, a mail client, a browser).

1. **Schedule.** Pull today's events: title, start, end, place and attendees. Flag overlaps as conflicts and gaps under 15 minutes as back to back.
2. **Mail.** Pull the unread messages and put each into exactly one group: urgent, needs reply, for information, newsletter. With more than 20 unread, do not read every body: give the counts and show only the top five urgent and needs-reply items ([[reference_how_to_triage_email]]).
3. **Weather,** one line, if the person's location is known: temperature, conditions, any alert.

## Delivering it

A fixed shape: a greeting line; the schedule as a table with conflicts and open blocks of 30 minutes or more called out; the inbox in its groups with counts; the weather line; and a short list of suggestions drawn only from what was actually seen (prepare a meeting, answer something urgent, block focus time).

Then stand by. Typical follow-ons: "draft a reply to X" goes to [[reference_how_to_write_an_email]], "prep me for X" to [[reference_how_to_handle_meetings_and_follow_ups]], "what does my week look like" to [[reference_how_to_optimize_calendar]].

## What not to do

- Never invent calendar or email content; report only what was read.
- If a mail or calendar call fails, say so and still deliver the part that came back. If no tool is connected, say that.
- The briefing never acts: it reports and suggests. Sending a draft or changing an event needs an explicit yes.
- Keep it short: an executive briefing, not a transcript.
