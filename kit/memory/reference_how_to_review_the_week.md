# How to run a weekly review

Use the person's mail and calendar tools (whatever is connected: an MCP server, a mail client, a browser). Pull the raw data first: calendar events from Monday to today, mail received this week, and mail sent this week. It works best on a Friday afternoon or at the weekend, when the week is done.

## The look back

- **From the calendar:** meetings attended, hours in meetings, the busiest and the lightest day, meetings cancelled or declined.
- **From email:** counts received and sent, the five senders who asked for the most attention, threads still waiting for the person's reply, and sent mail with no answer yet (follow-up candidates, [[reference_how_to_track_follow_ups]]).
- **From both:** action items in email that were never dealt with, follow-ups promised in a meeting, and deadlines mentioned this week that are still open.

## The week ahead

Pull next week's calendar, Monday to Friday. Flag the meetings that need preparation ([[reference_how_to_handle_meetings_and_follow_ups]]), note the focus time actually available ([[reference_how_to_optimize_calendar]]), and call out any deadline or important date.

## The report

1. A small table of numbers: meetings, meeting hours, emails received and sent, busiest and lightest day.
2. The three to five most important conversations or meetings, one line each.
3. Unfinished business as a checklist: action items, follow-ups, pending replies.
4. Follow-ups needed, by person, subject and date.
5. Next week as a day-by-day table with a preparation flag, total focus time and key deadlines.
6. Specific recommendations for the week ahead, never generic ones.

## What not to do

- If a limit of the mail or calendar tool stops a full pull, say so; never present partial data as complete.
- For email counts, use the search result counts instead of reading every body. Hand heavy summarizing (50 emails or more) to a helper.
- Every section must point at something the person can decide to do next, not only describe the week.
