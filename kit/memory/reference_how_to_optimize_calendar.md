# How to optimize a calendar

Use the person's calendar tools (whatever is connected: an MCP server, a calendar client, a browser). Pull the current week, Monday to Friday, and the free slots of 30 minutes or more. If the person named a date range, use that range instead. If no calendar tool is connected, say so and ask for the events.

## What to look for

For each day, check for:

- overlapping events: two things at the same time;
- back-to-back meetings: less than 15 minutes between them;
- stacks: three or more in a row with no break at all;
- anything before 8:00 or after 18:00.

Look for blocks of two or more free hours as focus time, and flag any day with none. Meeting load is the share of the 9-to-5 day spent in meetings; all-day events and out-of-office blocks are left out of that figure. If events are at different places, allow travel time between them.

List every recurring event of the week and flag those that look low-value: no description, no agenda, many attendees with few acceptances. For each, ask whether it could be an email instead.

## The report

1. A day-by-day table: events, meeting hours, focus hours and a status word such as balanced or heavy.
2. Conflicts and warnings by day and time.
3. The free focus blocks by day, with any day that has none.
4. The recurring-event audit, with the "could this be an email" question where it applies.
5. Specific, actionable suggestions, never generic ones.

Then offer next steps instead of taking them: block focus time on a named day and time, check whether a conflicting event can be moved, find a better slot for one event.

## What not to do

- These are suggestions; the person decides what is acted on.
- Never move or create an event without saying what will change and getting an explicit yes ([[reference_how_to_handle_meetings_and_follow_ups]]).
- Never invent a conflict or a free block that was not in the data pulled.
